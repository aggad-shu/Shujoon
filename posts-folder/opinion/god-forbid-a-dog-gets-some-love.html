God Forbid a Dog Gets Some Love

Yuri Al-Sawair - 17/11/2026 - Wadi Musa

This is written in reply to Hamza Al-Aqqad’s “Dogs in the West”, read it here.

A small dog in a purse, a dog eating a pig’s ear, a dog sleeping in its owners’ bed, and a dog with 5 million followers… Are we really babying a creature who would leave us for half a bite of food scraps? Hamza’s piece on dogs in the west has some solid points, but I do think doctor Melfi would have a field day with his resentment. It’s less a question of unconditional love and more a question of loyalty. “You get what you give” is a good saying about justice, but is not too accurate as a self-standing rule; precisely, you’d just hear it when someone does something bad. If you do something nice for a human you’re more likely to hear: “you give them a hand and they take the whole arm”.

But not for dogs

A dog’s loyalty is the perfect example of “You get what you give”, on both sides of the equation. You give it negligence, you get disobedience. You give it aggression, you get violence. You give it distrust, you get manipulation. However, if you give it training, love and food, you get a loyal, well-behaved, and distinguished gentleman (image below).

As per stray dogs, the rule holds the same, they are a reflection of how humans treat the species around them. First generation stray dogs, dogs that have been abandoned, are just a result of what happens when you adopt/buy a creature but choose to abandon it for a small imperfection. Which usually is a result of negligence. Second generation and more, are the transcended product of this human negligence. What do you expect when buying a dog that been ripped away from its family and sold in a plastic bag?

Dogs’ ethical flaw

Dogs are not the naive-to-a-fake-throw creature after all. They are what once was a wild animal that decided to join its enemy. This wild animal shared its land with a two-legged spear-clutching species, who was not so nice. With a cunning manoeuvre of using their waggly tails and puppy eyes, they abandoned their will to be wild and free to befriend their enemy. I may be sensationalizing the scenario, but I like to imagine that there once was a wolf who decided to turn its back on its pack, and abandoned their aspiration of becoming the alpha predators, to suck up to the ruthless and greedy human.

After all, that was still a commendable risk, what if getting too close would have gotten it an arrow to its ribs? Sorry for the graphic image, maybe I could have gone for a softer example. Anyways, this is my advice, Hamza: Adopt that cat.

  1. You will like the fact that they do not like to be babied
  2. It will help you with your little mouse problem
  3. You both hold resentment towards the same species.
