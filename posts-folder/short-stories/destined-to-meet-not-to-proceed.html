Destined to Meet, Not to Proceed

Baraa Mreh - 19/11/2026 - Tamra

The spring breeze carefully swayed the stalks of colorful flowers flanking the hill, guiding them into synchronized motion under its will. As he ascended the hill, the wind danced through his brunette hair, while his eyes hurriedly searched for her among the wandering crowd at the pavement below. It does not take him long to spot her, for her shining burgundy hair is hard to miss. In excitement, he secretly picks up a red Anemone flower from his side, puts it in his side pocket, and heads down towards her.

She was sitting on the sidewalk bench reading her book, blissfully ignoring all pedestrians and dog walkers. She was lost in her imagination, to the point where her countenance began shifting unconsciously. And even forgot that she was supposed to meet him minutes ago at their usual spot.

When he was approaching her, a soft smile wore his face carrying the enthusiasm from days. He was trying to get a hold of her from the moment he heard she was back in town. They were neighbors, childhood friends, classmates, and dear-to-the-heart mates.

“Haaloo” he said playfully from behind her.

“Ohh! Hey you!” she responded beamingly “you startled me.”

“Sorry about that, but you missed our planned meeting at the usual spot. I was sure you wouldn’t bail on me, so I decided to look for you.” He said confidently.

“Indeed. I would not bail on you dear friend. What are you hiding in your pocket?” she asked curiously while looking at his hand inside the pocket.

“Always rushing Nadia” he said while shaking his head grinning. “Have some patience, will you?”

“No. Not until you disclose your precious item.” She shut her book signaling for him to speak.

“Fine fine, I yield. And since we are already late, let us go straight into business.” He spoke. “But first I need to know why you left last time without paying me farewell?”

“You were occupied.” She said plainly, her face paying no heed to his.

“You could have sent your goodbyes with Faris, you are aware I see him at work” he replied.

The silence was loud between them, for Nadia knew he wasn’t mistaken. Suddenly, a shouting call to Nader came from afar interrupted the quietness, and they both knew this was an indication for him to leave. Nader looked behind towards the source of the noise, Faris was waving furiously but toned it down a bit once he saw Nader’s company. Right when he turned his head to face her, she rushed her words out.

“I..” she stuttered, and he paused. “I was frightened that it might be the last time I’d see you before returning to college…abroad” she completed in a wistful tone, her eyes not daring to meet his. “So, when you were absent, I took it upon myself that once I return home again, I’ll come see you first.”

In the hopes of ignoring her regretful tone, she waited patiently for him to reach for his pocket to disclose his mystery. When he read her staring eyes on his front pocket, a red lively flower came out, swirling between his thumb and index fingers.

“First, we are here together now, so you can put your concern at rest” he said. “Second, this is for you” handing her the flower. “So, for next time you remember to come by and bid me farewell properly,” he added teasingly. Faris shouted one more time, nervously but urgently, “this is my signal to go,” pointing at his friend “do not, ever Nadia, be a stranger” he requested with a wide smile on his face, then left.

Nadia was speechless, fond of, and extremely touched by his words. She placed the red flower between the pages of her book and sat down to recollect her composure. Her eyes were tracing him while he was slowly fading among the crowd.

Another gust sent her hair flying back, flipped open her paper book, and before she realized, it carried the red flower too. She remained stationary, motionless, and controlled by her feelings. Despite their brief collision, his presence’s impact lingered. With the red, fleeting souvenir, her ephemeral emotions shifted swiftly from elation to grief - like a passing spring breeze.
